## Selected Publications

1. **2026.** [A Large-Area Optical Time Projection Chamber for Hard X-ray Polarimetry with Directional Imaging of Low-Energy Electron Recoils](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26239).  
   Optical TPCs go astrophysical. Same detector instincts, different sky-facing hat.

2. **2026.** [Modeling the light response of an optically readout GEM-based TPC for the CYGNO experiment](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-15318-7).  
   Turning detector light into a response model, because photons are adorable but not naturally organized.

3. **2025.** [Study on discharge and short-circuit generation in CMS GE1/1 triple-GEM detectors during Run 3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/20/05/P05035).  
   A practical look at what GEM detectors do when they are under pressure and have opinions.

4. **2025.** [Single-channel PICOSEC Micromegas detector with improved time resolution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nima.2024.170127).  
   Fast timing, but make it faster.

5. **2025.** [Photocathode characterisation for robust PICOSEC Micromegas precise-timing detectors](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nima.2024.170151).  
   The photocathode gets a personality assessment, professionally.

6. **2024.** [Enhancing the light yield of He:CF4-based gaseous detector](https://dx.doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-024-13471-5).  
   More light from gas, which is exactly as suspicious and useful as it sounds.

7. **2024.** [A novel diamond-like carbon based photocathode for PICOSEC Micromegas detectors](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/19/08/P08010).  
   Detector materials doing the elegant little dance between robustness and performance.

8. **2024.** [Charge amplification in low-pressure CF4:SF6:He mixtures with a multi-mesh THGEM for directional Dark Matter searches](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/19/06/P06021).  
   Low-pressure gas mixtures for the noble sport of chasing directional Dark Matter.

9. **2024.** [Development of the CMS detector for the CERN LHC Run 3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/19/05/P05064).  
   Big detector, big collaboration, many subsystems, no small amount of coordination.

10. **2023.** [Impact of magnetic field on the stability of the CMS GE1/1 GEM detector operation](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/18/11/P11029).  
    Magnetic fields: useful, dramatic, and occasionally rude to detectors.

11. **2023.** [Towards a muon collider](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-023-11889-x).  
    The future collider idea that arrives with physics ambition and background levels that require respect.

12. **2021.** [Quality control of mass-produced GEM detectors for the CMS GE1/1 muon upgrade](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nima.2022.166716).  
    Making many GEM detectors and convincing them all to be grown-ups.

13. **2021.** [Performance of a triple-GEM demonstrator in pp collisions at the CMS detector](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/16/11/P11014).  
    A demonstrator leaves the bench and meets the real collision environment.

14. **2020.** [Triple-GEM discharge probability studies at CHARM: simulations and experimental results](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/15/10/P10013).  
    Simulations, tests, and the eternal question: how often does the detector decide to zap?

15. **2020.** [Interstrip capacitances of the readout board used in large triple-GEM detectors for the CMS Muon Upgrade](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/15/12/P12019).  
    A reminder that readout boards have inner lives.

16. **2020.** [Performance of prototype GE1/1 chambers for the CMS muon spectrometer upgrade](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nima.2020.164104).  
    Prototype chambers on the road to becoming serious detector infrastructure.
