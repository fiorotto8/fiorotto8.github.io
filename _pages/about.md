I am an experimental particle and astroparticle physicist who builds detectors
that try to catch extremely shy signals without frightening them away. The
official version is: gaseous detectors, Micro-Pattern Gaseous Detectors,
optical time projection chambers, X-ray polarimetry, and fast-timing
technologies. The field version is: hardware, software, gas, light, cables,
simulation, and a suspicious amount of coffee.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the Gran Sasso Science Institute
and Technical Coordinator of the CYGNO directional Dark Matter experiment at
LNGS. My work follows experiments through the full lifecycle: physics case,
detector concept, construction, simulation, reconstruction, commissioning, data
taking, operations, and the delicate art of making complicated systems behave
as if they had read the manual.

## Current Work

- Leading the transition from the LIME R&D prototype to CYGNO04, a 400 liter
  radiopure demonstrator for directional Dark Matter searches.
- Developing optical-readout GEM TPCs for hard X-ray polarimetry in the HypeX
  research line.
- Coordinating optical readout for CYGNUS detector concepts and in-orbit
  background simulations for the EXPO ESA M8 mission proposal.
- Keeping one foot in detector operations and the other in analysis and
  simulation, which is a very academic way to stand.
