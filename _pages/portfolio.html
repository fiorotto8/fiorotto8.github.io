My research sits in the slightly noisy intersection between detector hardware, simulation, project management, and operations. The common thread is rare-event instrumentation: building detectors that are stable, useful, scalable, and only occasionally interested in drama.

CYGNO04: building an ERC-funded dark matter detector

CYGNO is my current center of gravity: an ERC-funded program developing optical-readout gaseous TPCs for directional Dark Matter searches. I am the technical coordinator and project manager of CYGNO04, a 400 liter, EUR 1M detector that I am managing from design through procurement, assembly, installation, and commissioning. So yes, the detector has a budget, a schedule, and a personality.

  • Technical coordination and project management for the CYGNO04 demonstrator.
  • Optical and electronic readout strategy, light-yield studies, and saturation mitigation.
  • Gas-mixture development, negative-ion-drift R&D, and future large-volume concepts.
  • Geant4 background studies, detector-response modelling, timelines, action tracking, and the larger CYGNUS path.

Optical TPCs for X-ray polarimetry

X-ray polarimetry is a research line on its own. I adapt optical-readout triple-GEM TPC technology to reconstruct photoelectron tracks and extract polarization information in an energy range where the tracks are small, fragile, and not especially cooperative. The current activity is supported by the PRIN-funded HypeX effort, but the detector idea is broader than that project label.

  • Large-area triple-GEM optical TPC development and laboratory testing.
  • Photoelectron-track reconstruction and modulation-factor studies.
  • Detector concepts for polarimetric sensitivity around the 10-60 keV range.
  • Simulation, calibration, and data-MC sanity checks for optical polarimetric response.

PICOSEC timing for future muon colliders

For Muon Collider detector studies, I coordinated the PICOSEC Micromegas R&D line. The challenge is to move toward larger, robust detector concepts while preserving sub-100 ps timing performance in environments where backgrounds arrive with theatrical confidence.

  • Prototype development from small devices toward larger-area concepts.
  • Photocathode, Cherenkov radiator, gas-mixture, and rate-capability studies.
  • Beam-induced-background simulation and timing-based rejection strategies.
  • R&D coordination across hardware choices, test-beam planning, simulation needs, and the eternal spreadsheet.

CMS GEM detectors, from R&D to operations

My CMS GEM work focused on triple-GEM detectors for the CMS muon system: R&D, quality control, commissioning, gas-system coordination, and operation in real LHC conditions. This is where detector elegance meets industrial reality and both are asked to be stable by Monday.

  • Aging, discharge, rate-capability, and magnetic-field stability studies.
  • Gas-system monitoring and L3 responsibility within the CMS GEM structure.
  • Test beams, commissioning, Detector-On-Call shifts, DCS shifts, and data-quality tools.
  • Operational memory, safety interfaces, service coordination, and the noble aim of making complex systems look boring.

New R&D: neutrinos, space, and CEvNS

I also keep a small R&D corner for ideas that are young, promising, and only slightly feral. The line connects detector simulation, astroparticle instrumentation, and new ways of squeezing directional information out of difficult signals.

  • Directional solar-neutrino studies with Super-Kamiokande.
  • EXPO satellite project: instrument-team member and head of space-environment simulation.
  • Novel CEvNS detection concepts using high-pressure CF4 TPCs.
